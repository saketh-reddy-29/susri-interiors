Structuring the Physical Archivefor Future Architectural Studies

Every commission leaves behind more than a building. Off-cuts, failed castings, and stone samples accumulate into a physical record of the studio’s thinking. This year we began the slow work of cataloguing that record into a permanent, consultable archive.

The Case for a Physical Record

Digital libraries flatten a material into an image. A photograph of travertine cannot convey its weight, its temperature, or the drag of a fingertip across an unfilled void. The archive exists because the decisions we make are tactile, and tactile decisions demand tactile evidence.

Cataloguing by Behaviour

We refuse to index the archive by supplier or by name. Samples are grouped by how they behave over time: what darkens, what chalks, what accepts a scratch and holds it. A designer searching the shelves is searching for a future condition, not a present colour.

Failed Castings Are Kept

Roughly a third of the shelving is given over to work that did not succeed. A bronze pull that cooled unevenly, a plaster panel that cracked along an unexpected axis. These objects teach far more quickly than the finished pieces, and the studio consults them constantly.

The Shelf as Instrument

The archive is housed on a single 14-metre run of raw steel shelving, deliberately placed beside the north windows. Every sample sits in the same flat, consistent northern light, which makes honest comparison possible between materials sourced a decade apart.

Accession and Refusal

Nothing enters the archive automatically. Each proposed addition is reviewed and must demonstrate something the collection does not already hold. The discipline of refusal that governs our interiors governs the archive with equal severity.

Dating Every Surface

Each sample carries a small engraved date on its reverse. Returning to a limestone offcut eleven years after it was quarried tells us precisely what a decade of ambient light and handling does to that specific bed. Without the date, the object is merely decorative.

Access Beyond the Studio

From next spring the archive will be open by appointment to students and practitioners. The collection is not proprietary knowledge; it is an accumulation of observations that becomes more valuable the more people are permitted to consult it.

Building for the Next Custodian

We are structuring the archive for a generation that has not yet joined the studio. The naming, the dating, and the deliberate documentation of failure are all written for someone who will never meet the architect who made the original decision.
